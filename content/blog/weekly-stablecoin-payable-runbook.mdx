A weekly stablecoin payable run is the recurring cycle an operations or treasury team uses to turn a week's worth of approved invoices into executed stablecoin payments — reliably, auditable, and without last-minute scrambling. The core sequence is: build the batch from approved invoices, check balances and wallet fields, complete maker-checker release, send remittance, and hand the evidence package to accounting. Each step exists to catch a specific failure mode — stale wallet data, duplicate entries, insufficient balances, or missing audit trails — before it becomes a payment error.

This runbook assumes your approval roles already exist. It does not redesign who can approve what; it describes the operational sequence a team follows once those roles are in place. It also does not prescribe how large a liquidity buffer you should hold — that is a treasury policy decision outside the scope of a weekly payable checklist.

## Why a structured weekly stablecoin payable run matters

Ad hoc crypto payments — one invoice sent whenever someone remembers — create two problems. First, there's no consistent review point, so errors in wallet address or network selection surface only after funds have moved. Second, there's no clean handoff to accounting, which makes month-end reconciliation slower and increases the chance that a payment gets recorded against the wrong invoice or vendor.

A weekly stablecoin payable run solves both problems by batching work into a single, repeatable cycle with defined checkpoints. Teams that already pay suppliers this way often reference a general process for how to [pay business invoices with USDT](/blog/how-to-pay-business-invoices-with-usdt/), then adapt it into a recurring weekly cadence rather than a one-off transaction.

## The five-step weekly stablecoin payable run

The following numbered sequence describes one full cycle, from batch creation to accounting handoff.

1. **Build the batch from approved invoices.** Pull every invoice that has cleared your existing approval workflow and is due for payment in the current cycle. Exclude anything still pending sign-off — the batch should only contain invoices that have already passed your internal controls.
2. **Check balances and wallet fields.** Confirm the paying wallet holds sufficient stablecoin balance for the full batch, including any network fee estimate. For each line item, verify the destination address, the network (e.g., Ethereum, Tron, or another supported chain), and the stablecoin denomination match what the vendor confirmed — not just what's stored from a previous payment.
3. **Complete maker-checker release.** The preparer submits the batch for review. The approver independently checks invoice references, amounts, and wallet fields against the maker's work before releasing funds. Neither role should also execute the release unsupervised — this split exists specifically to catch single-person error or manipulation.
4. **Send remittance.** Once released, execute the transfers and immediately log the transaction hash, timestamp, and network confirmation status for each payment. If you route larger batches through an over-the-counter desk for conversion first, that step typically happens before this stage — see how [OTC crypto desks](/blog/when-businesses-use-otc-crypto-desks/) are generally used for sizing conversions ahead of a payable cycle.
5. **Hand off evidence to accounting.** Package the release log, transaction hashes, invoice references, and fiat-equivalent values at time of settlement, then transfer this evidence to accounting for reconciliation against the general ledger and vendor subledger.

## Building the AP calendar around the run

A weekly stablecoin payable run works best when it sits inside a fixed AP calendar rather than floating from week to week. A predictable calendar gives vendors a consistent expectation and gives your maker-checker team a fixed review window instead of a rushed, ad hoc one.

| Day | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Pull approved invoices, build draft batch | AP preparer (maker) |
| Tuesday | Verify wallet fields, balances, and network selection | AP preparer (maker) |
| Wednesday | Independent review and maker-checker release | Approver (checker) |
| Thursday | Send remittance, log transaction hashes | Payments ops |
| Friday | Package evidence and hand off to accounting | Payments ops / accounting liaison |

This table is a starting template — your own AP calendar may compress these steps into fewer days or extend the review window for larger batches. The point is consistency: the same days, the same order, every cycle.

## The release log: your audit backbone

Every weekly stablecoin payable run should produce a release log — a structured record of what was approved, what was sent, and what accounting received. At minimum, a release log entry should capture:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Invoice reference | Links the payment to the original approved invoice |
| Vendor wallet address | Confirms destination used for this specific payment |
| Network / chain | Prevents cross-chain misdirection errors |
| Stablecoin and amount | Matches what was approved during release |
| Transaction hash | Provides on-chain proof of settlement |
| Maker / checker names | Documents segregation of duties for audit |
| Fiat-equivalent value at settlement | Supports accounting conversion entries |

Keeping this log consistent week over week is what turns a one-off payment into an auditable weekly stablecoin payable run rather than a series of disconnected transfers. Teams weighing which stablecoin to standardize on for this log often start from a comparison like [USDC vs USDT for supplier payments](/blog/usdc-vs-usdt-for-supplier-payments/) before locking in a default denomination for the cycle.

## Risks and limits of a weekly stablecoin payable run

A structured runbook reduces — but does not eliminate — operational risk. Several limits are worth stating plainly:

- **Wallet errors are irreversible.** Unlike a bank wire recall request, a stablecoin transfer sent to an incorrect address or wrong network typically cannot be reversed. The balance and wallet-field check in step two exists specifically to reduce this risk, not eliminate it.
- **Network conditions vary.** Confirmation times and fees differ by chain and can change during periods of network congestion, which may affect your Thursday remittance timing.
- **Fiat-equivalent values fluctuate.** The value you log at settlement time may differ from the value at invoice issuance, which has accounting and reporting implications your finance team should address under your own policies and applicable standards — see general context from [IFRS](https://www.ifrs.org) on digital asset accounting treatment.
- **Compliance obligations do not disappear.** KYB, sanctions screening, and counterparty due diligence remain necessary regardless of payment rail. Review relevant guidance from [FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org) and, where applicable, regional frameworks such as the EU's MiCA regime.
- **This runbook does not replace treasury policy.** Decisions about how much stablecoin balance to hold, which networks to support, or how to size conversions sit with your treasury function — see [corporate crypto treasury basics](/blog/corporate-crypto-treasury-basics/) for that broader context.

No step in this runbook guarantees a particular financial outcome, and none of it should be read as investment, legal, or tax advice.

## Weekly payable checklist

Use this checklist as a quick pre-release gate before any weekly stablecoin payable run goes out the door:

1. All invoices in the batch have cleared existing approval workflow.
2. Paying wallet balance covers total batch amount plus estimated network fees.
3. Every destination wallet address and network has been re-verified against the vendor's confirmed details.
4. Maker and checker roles are distinct individuals, and the checker has independently reviewed every line item.
5. Transaction hashes and timestamps are logged immediately after each transfer.
6. The evidence package — release log, hashes, invoice references, fiat-equivalent values — is ready for accounting handoff before the end of the cycle.
7. Any failed or flagged transaction has been isolated, documented, and scheduled for separate resubmission.

Running this checklist every cycle is what keeps a weekly stablecoin payable run repeatable rather than reinvented each time.
